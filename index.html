Executive Overview

Connecting Policy to the Rural Household

AARAMBH 1000 serves as the human layer between institutional healthcare policy (Mission Poshan 2.0 & NHM) and everyday household practices during the window that defines human brain growth.

01

The Critical Window

Over 80% of brain architecture forms before age two. Nutrition alone is not enough—responsive caregiving, play stimulation, and maternal mental health are non-negotiable pillars.

02

The Human Layer

Rather than creating parallel systems, NGO field facilitators mobilize Anganwadi centers, assist ASHA visits, and empower mothers directly in their home environments.

03

Maternal-Child Nexus

A child cannot thrive if the mother is exhausted or emotionally distressed. We directly integrate postpartum depression screenings and emotional support circles.

Evidence & Analytics

Baseline Indicators & 24-Month Ambitions

National NFHS-5 indicators establish the urgent rationale, while AARAMBH 1000 sets targeted, empirical benchmarks across 10 pilot villages.

Methodological Note: National NFHS-5 indicators establish the structural rationale. Each of the 10 pilot villages undergoes a localized Baseline Assessment (T₀) to establish empirical village-specific starting benchmarks, followed by mid-line (T₁) and endline (T₂) evaluation.

Implementation Timeline

24-Month Pilot Roadmap

Structured rollout phases designed for rigorous village implementation and impact evaluation.

Months 01–03

Phase I: Preparation & Baseline Survey (T₀)

Village selection based on criteria, stakeholder orientation, household mapping, baseline survey implementation, and training package adaptation.

Months 04–06

Phase II: Capacity Building & Launch

Joint workshops for NGO facilitators, ASHAs, and AWWs. Community inception meetings and initial household registration.

Months 07–18

Phase III: Full Implementation

Monthly Aarambh Village Days, Mother Circles, Father Workshops, home visits, growth monitoring, and continuous referral tracking.

Months 19–21

Phase IV: Consolidation & Mid-Course Refinement

Mid-term evaluation (T₁), identifying coverage gaps, intensifying support for high-risk mother-child pairs.

Months 22–24

Phase V: Endline Evaluation (T₂) & Scale-Up Plan

Comprehensive endline survey, dissemination of learnings to health departments, and formulation of expansion model (10 → 50 villages).

Ethical Guardrails

Programmatic Non-Negotiables

Grounded in public health ethics, AARAMBH 1000 maintains strict principles to protect rural families.

No Parallel System

We strengthen existing ASHAs, ANMs, and AWCs rather than replacing public infrastructure.

Zero App Dependency

Mothers interact via human care and physical Family Cards; no smartphone or digital access required.

No Unverified Foods

We promote locally available, affordable foods and government-sanctioned nutritional guidelines.

No Medical Diagnosis

Facilitators screen and refer; medical diagnoses are strictly conducted by certified health officers.

Zero Mother Shaming

Empathy-first counseling supporting mothers in their unique circumstances without judgment.

No "Genius Baby" Claims

Focus is on developmental rights, physical health, and emotional security—not false academic promises.
